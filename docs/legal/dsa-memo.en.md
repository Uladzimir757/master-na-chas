# Memo: ZR and the Digital Services Act (DSA) — status and next steps

*Internal/working memo, not a public document. Not legal advice — to be confirmed with counsel, especially once the company is registered and as ZR scales.*

## Why Booksy publishes what you saw (DSA notice, Trust Center)

Booksy is a large platform (over 5.6M monthly active recipients in Poland, per the figures you quoted) — it falls under the full DSA scope for "online platforms," including:
- publishing the average number of monthly active recipients (DSA Art. 24(2)),
- a public point of contact for users and authorities (DSA Art. 11-12),
- periodic transparency reports (DSA Art. 15, 24),
- a Trust Center as an additional, voluntary trust-building measure (not a formal DSA requirement — Booksy's own initiative).

## Does this apply to ZR now

**Most likely not in full — but not "zero obligations" either.** The DSA has two tiers:

1. **Baseline obligations for ALL intermediary services** (Chapter III, Section 1) — these already apply to ZR regardless of size:
   - clear basic service information in the Terms (already covered by `terms.en.md`),
   - a point of contact for users and authorities — the contact address in the Terms/Privacy Policy is sufficient,
   - since ZR hosts user content (Client reviews count as "hosting" under the DSA) — a notice-and-action mechanism for illegal content in reviews — **to add**: a short procedure for reporting unlawful reviews (see `terms.en.md` §6, to be expanded with a formal reporting channel).

2. **Section 4 obligations (Art. 24-27, including the recipient-count report under Art. 24(2))** — apply only to "online platforms" (services enabling public dissemination of content — which Client reviews likely qualify as) **excluding micro and small enterprises** (DSA Art. 19 — fewer than 50 employees and turnover/balance sheet ≤€10M), unless they are a "very large online platform" (VLOP, >45M EU recipients — obviously not ZR).

**Conclusion:** ZR, as a pre-registration startup with no revenue and a handful of users, almost certainly qualifies as a micro-enterprise and is **exempt from the recipient-count reporting duty (Art. 24(2))** and from the rest of Section 4 (recommender-system and ad transparency, etc. — moot anyway, since ZR shows no ads and has no recommender system in that sense).

## What to do now (minimal scope)

1. Terms and Privacy Policy include a point of contact — **done** (fill in a real email address).
2. Add a short illegal-content/review reporting mechanism to the Terms (who to contact, how, response timeframe) — an extension of §6.
3. **Do not publish** a Booksy-style "Digital Services Act" notice at this stage — it would be misleading (implying an obligation you don't have) and would require counting real "active recipients," which you don't need to do.

## What to do later (once the company grows)

- Once you cross the micro-enterprise threshold (>50 employees or >€10M turnover/balance sheet) — check with counsel the full scope of DSA Section 4, including publishing the average monthly EU active-recipient count, similar to the Booksy document you shared.
- If the platform starts enabling distance contracts between consumers and registered "traders" (e.g. once Masters formally register a business and ZR starts collecting their registration data) — review DSA Art. 30 (trader traceability) — also covered by the micro/small-enterprise exemption, but worth keeping on the radar.
- Consider a voluntary "Trust Center" (like Booksy's) once the number of Masters and Clients is large enough for it to build real trust (unnecessary overhead for now).
